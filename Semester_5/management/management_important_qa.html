Management Principles — Complete Question Bank

Group A — Short Answer Questions (1 Mark Each)

Q1Define Management.

Ans: Management is the process of getting things done with the aim of achieving organizational goals effectively and efficiently by coordinating and directing the efforts of people.

Q2List the four main functions of management.

Ans: The four main functions are: Planning, Organizing, Staffing, Directing, Controlling. Coordination is sometimes listed as a separate fifth function that binds the others together.

Q3Name the three levels of management.

Ans: The three levels are: Top-level (Board of Directors, CEO), Middle-level (Functional/Departmental Managers), and Lower-level / Operational-level (Supervisors, Foremen).

Q4What is the Scientific Management Theory?

Ans: Scientific Management, proposed by F.W. Taylor (Father of Scientific Management), emphasizes using scientific methods, time-and-motion studies, and standardization to improve labor productivity and efficiency.

Q5What are Henri Fayol's 14 principles of management?

Ans: Division of work, Authority & responsibility, Discipline, Unity of command, Unity of direction, Subordination of individual interest, Remuneration, Centralization, Scalar chain, Order, Equity, Stability, Initiative, Esprit de corps.

Q6Who is known as the Father of Bureaucracy?

Ans: Max Weber, a German sociologist, is known as the Father of Bureaucracy. He proposed a structured, formal, rule-based organization with clear hierarchy and specialization.

Q7What is the Hawthorne Effect?

Ans: The Hawthorne Effect refers to the phenomenon where workers' productivity increases when they receive special attention from researchers or managers — it showed that social and psychological factors affect worker output.

Q8Define Planning.

Ans: Planning is the primary function of management that involves setting objectives and determining the courses of action to be followed to achieve those objectives.

Q9Name the three types of plans based on scope.

Ans: Based on scope: Strategic plans (long-term, organization-wide), Tactical plans (medium-term, departmental), and Operational plans (short-term, day-to-day).

Q10What is SWOT Analysis?

Ans: SWOT stands for Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats. It is a strategic planning tool used to identify internal capabilities (S, W) and external environmental factors (O, T) of an organization.

Q11What is PEST Analysis?

Ans: PEST stands for Political, Economic, Social, Technological factors. It is a tool used to analyze the macro-environmental factors affecting an organization.

Q12What are Porter's Five Forces?

Ans: Porter's Five Forces are: (i) Threat of new entrants, (ii) Bargaining power of suppliers, (iii) Bargaining power of buyers, (iv) Threat of substitute products, (v) Rivalry among existing competitors.

Q13Define Organizing.

Ans: Organizing is the management function that involves arranging resources and tasks in a structured way — defining roles, grouping activities, and establishing reporting relationships to achieve objectives.

Q14What is Span of Control?

Ans: Span of Control refers to the number of subordinates a manager can effectively supervise. It can be wide (flat) or narrow (tall) depending on the complexity of work.

Q15What is Departmentalization?

Ans: Departmentalization is the process of grouping organizational activities/jobs into departments based on function, product, geography, customer, or process to improve coordination.

Q16Define Delegation of Authority.

Ans: Delegation is the process by which a manager transfers part of his/her authority and responsibility to a subordinate, while the manager retains ultimate accountability.

Q17Distinguish between Centralization and Decentralization.

Ans: Centralization — decision-making authority concentrated at top levels. Decentralization — authority dispersed to lower levels. The right balance depends on organization size and environment.

Q18What is a Matrix Organization?

Ans: A Matrix Organization is a structure where employees report to both a functional manager and a project manager, combining functional and project-based reporting lines.

Q19Define Staffing.

Ans: Staffing is the management function involving recruitment, selection, training, development, and appraisal of human resources to fill positions in the organization.

Q20What is Human Resource Planning?

Ans: HR Planning is the process of forecasting future manpower requirements and ensuring the right number of people with the right skills are available at the right time.

Q21What is Directing?

Ans: Directing is the management function that guides, motivates, leads, and supervises subordinates to achieve organizational goals — initiating action and providing leadership.

Q22Define Motivation.

Ans: Motivation is the process of stimulating people to act in a desired manner so as to accomplish desired goals; it is the inner drive that energizes behavior.

Q23Name the five levels of Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs.

Ans: (1) Physiological, (2) Safety, (3) Social/Belongingness, (4) Esteem, (5) Self-actualization — arranged in a pyramid from basic to highest needs.

Q24What is Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory?

Ans: Herzberg classified factors into Hygiene factors (salary, working conditions — prevent dissatisfaction) and Motivation factors (achievement, recognition — drive satisfaction).

Q25What are McGregor's Theory X and Theory Y?

Ans: Theory X assumes workers dislike work and must be controlled. Theory Y assumes workers are self-directed and seek responsibility — opposite managerial assumptions about people.

Q26Define Leadership.

Ans: Leadership is the ability to influence a group of individuals toward the achievement of goals; it involves setting direction and motivating people.

Q27Name three leadership styles.

Ans: Three common styles are: Autocratic (leader makes decisions alone), Democratic (consultative/participative), and Laissez-faire (hands-off, delegation-heavy).

Q28What is the Communication Process?

Ans: Communication involves a Sender → Encoding → Message → Channel → Decoding → Receiver → Feedback. Noise can distort the message at any stage.

Q29Define Controlling.

Ans: Controlling is the management function of monitoring performance, comparing it with standards, and taking corrective action to ensure goals are achieved as planned.

Q30What is Budgetary Control?

Ans: Budgetary Control is the technique of using budgets to plan, monitor, and control financial operations — comparing actual performance with budgeted figures and taking corrective steps.

Q31Define Corporate Governance.

Ans: Corporate Governance is the system of rules, practices, and processes by which a company is directed and controlled — ensuring accountability, transparency, and fairness to stakeholders.

Q32What is Organizational Culture?

Ans: Organizational Culture is the shared values, beliefs, norms, and artifacts that characterize an organization and influence the behavior of its members.

Q33Define Change Management.

Ans: Change Management is the structured approach to transitioning individuals, teams, and organizations from a current state to a desired future state.

Q34What is Organizational Development (OD)?

Ans: OD is a planned, systematic process of applying behavioral science principles to improve organizational effectiveness, health, and self-renewal.

Q35Define Conflict Management.

Ans: Conflict Management is the practice of identifying and handling conflicts sensibly, fairly, and efficiently — using techniques like negotiation, mediation, and collaboration.

Group B — Descriptive Questions (5 Marks Each)

Q1Explain the nature and scope of management. (5 Marks)

Ans:

Nature of Management:

  • Goal-oriented: Aimed at achieving predetermined objectives.
  • Continuous process: Functions recur in a cycle.
  • Pervasive: Applicable to all organizations (profit/non-profit).
  • Multidisciplinary: Draws from economics, psychology, sociology.
  • Dynamic: Adapts to changing environments.
  • Intangible: Cannot be seen but felt through results.

Scope of Management: Applies to all levels (top, middle, lower), all departments (production, finance, marketing, HR), and all types of organizations. Covers managing people, work, and resources.

Q2Describe the functions of management. (5 Marks)

Ans: The key functions of management are:

  1. Planning — Setting goals and deciding how to achieve them.
  2. Organizing — Arranging resources and tasks.
  3. Staffing — Recruiting and developing people.
  4. Directing — Leading, motivating, communicating.
  5. Controlling — Monitoring and correcting performance.
  6. Coordination — Harmonizing activities across departments.

These are interlinked: planning provides direction, staffing fills positions, directing activates people, controlling ensures goals are met, and coordination binds everything.

Q3Explain Henry Mintzberg's Managerial Roles. (5 Marks)

Ans: Mintzberg identified 10 roles in 3 groups:

  • Interpersonal: Figurehead, Leader, Liaison
  • Informational: Monitor, Disseminator, Spokesperson
  • Decisional: Entrepreneur, Disturbance handler, Resource allocator, Negotiator

These roles describe what managers actually do, rather than what they should do.

Q4Discuss F.W. Taylor's Principles of Scientific Management. (5 Marks)

Ans: Taylor's four principles:

  1. Science, not rule of thumb: Develop scientific methods for each task.
  2. Harmony, not discord: Cooperation between management and workers.
  3. Cooperation, not individualism: Group work over individual.
  4. Development of each person: Train workers to reach maximum efficiency.

Taylor also emphasized time-and-motion studies, standardization, and differential piece-wage system to link pay to output.

Q5Explain Fayol's 14 Principles of Management. (5 Marks)

Ans: Key principles include:

  • Division of work: Specialization increases efficiency.
  • Unity of command: One boss per subordinate.
  • Unity of direction: One head, one plan for activities with same objective.
  • Scalar chain: Clear line of authority from top to bottom.
  • Esprit de corps: Promote team spirit.

Other principles cover authority, discipline, order, equity, stability of tenure, initiative, and remuneration.

Q6Describe Max Weber's Bureaucratic Management. (5 Marks)

Ans: Weber's features of an ideal bureaucracy:

  • Clear hierarchy of authority
  • Division of labor based on functional specialization
  • Written rules and regulations
  • Impersonal relationships
  • Selection based on technical qualification
  • Career orientation for officials

Bureaucracy ensures efficiency, predictability, and consistency but may lead to rigidity and red tape.

Q7What are the steps in the Planning Process? (5 Marks)

Ans: Planning process steps:

  1. Setting objectives — Defining clear, achievable goals.
  2. Developing premises — Assumptions about the future environment.
  3. Identifying alternatives — Listing possible courses of action.
  4. Evaluating alternatives — Weighing pros and cons.
  5. Selecting an alternative — Choosing the best plan.
  6. Implementing the plan — Putting it into action.
  7. Follow-up — Monitoring and revising.
Q8Explain the types of decisions in management. (5 Marks)

Ans: Types of decisions:

  • Programmed vs Non-programmed: Routine/repetitive vs unique/strategic.
  • Strategic, Tactical, Operational: Long-term, medium-term, day-to-day.
  • Structured vs Unstructured: Clear procedure vs requires judgment.
  • Individual vs Group: Made alone vs collaboratively.
  • Major vs Minor: High impact vs low impact.
Q9Describe the Decision Making Process. (5 Marks)

Ans:

  1. Identify the problem
  2. Diagnose the problem (find root causes)
  3. Develop alternatives
  4. Evaluate alternatives
  5. Select the best alternative
  6. Implement the decision
  7. Follow-up and evaluate

Decision-making can be rational (logical), bounded rational (limited info), or intuitive.

Q10Explain the types of organizational structures. (5 Marks)

Ans: Common organizational structures:

  • Functional: Groups by function (Marketing, Finance, Production).
  • Divisional: Groups by product, geography, or customer.
  • Matrix: Dual reporting — functional + project managers.
  • Network: Core organization outsources non-core functions.
  • Team-based: Self-managed teams handle work processes.
  • Line and Staff: Line authority with staff specialists.
Q11Discuss the importance of Delegation of Authority. (5 Marks)

Ans: Delegation is important because it:

  • Reduces manager's workload
  • Develops subordinates' skills and confidence
  • Facilitates quicker decisions at lower levels
  • Improves coordination and specialization
  • Builds a pipeline of future managers
  • Allows top management to focus on strategic issues

Barriers to delegation include fear of loss of control, lack of trust, and absence of reward systems.

Q12Distinguish between Line and Staff authority. (5 Marks)

Ans:

  • Line authority: Direct command relationship — managers can issue orders to subordinates. Exists in production, sales.
  • Staff authority: Advisory role — staff experts advise line managers. Exists in HR, legal, R&D.

Line managers achieve primary objectives; staff managers provide support and specialized expertise.

Q13Explain the steps in the Recruitment and Selection process. (5 Marks)

Ans:

Recruitment steps: Identify vacancy → Job analysis → Job description → Choose source (internal/external) → Attract candidates.

Selection steps: Screening applications → Preliminary interview → Selection tests → Selection interview → Reference & background checks → Medical examination → Final selection → Appointment letter.

Selection aims to choose the best-fit candidate for the role.

Q14What is Performance Appraisal? List its methods. (5 Marks)

Ans: Performance Appraisal is the systematic evaluation of an employee's job performance and potential.

Methods:

  • Traditional: Ranking, Graphic Rating Scale, Critical Incident, Essay, Forced Distribution.
  • Modern: MBO (Management by Objectives), 360-Degree Feedback, BARS (Behaviorally Anchored Rating Scale), Assessment Centre.
Q15Explain Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs Theory. (5 Marks)

Ans: Maslow arranged needs in a hierarchy (lowest to highest):

  1. Physiological: Food, water, shelter, sleep.
  2. Safety: Job security, stability, protection.
  3. Social: Love, friendship, belonging.
  4. Esteem: Recognition, status, respect.
  5. Self-actualization: Personal growth, achieving potential.

A person is motivated by the lowest unsatisfied need. Once satisfied, the next higher need takes over.

Q16Describe Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory of Motivation. (5 Marks)

Ans: Herzberg classified work factors into two categories:

  • Hygiene factors (extrinsic): Salary, working conditions, company policies, supervision. Their absence causes dissatisfaction but their presence does not motivate.
  • Motivation factors (intrinsic): Achievement, recognition, responsibility, growth. These drive satisfaction and motivation.

Managers must ensure hygiene factors are adequate and provide motivation factors to drive performance.

Q17Explain the Contingency Theory of Leadership (Fiedler). (5 Marks)

Ans: Fiedler proposed that effective leadership depends on the match between leader style and situational favorableness.

  • Leader-Member Relations — trust and confidence
  • Task Structure — clarity of tasks
  • Position Power — authority to reward/punish

Task-oriented leaders work best in very favorable or very unfavorable situations; relationship-oriented leaders work best in moderately favorable situations.

Q18What are the barriers to effective communication? (5 Marks)

Ans: Major barriers to communication:

  • Semantic barriers: Words mean different things to different people.
  • Physical barriers: Noise, distance, faulty equipment.
  • Psychological barriers: Emotions, perceptions, biases.
  • Cultural barriers: Differences in values and norms.
  • Organizational barriers: Hierarchical structure, faulty communication channels.
  • Personal barriers: Lack of attention, poor listening skills.
Q19Describe the controlling process. (5 Marks)

Ans: The controlling process involves:

  1. Setting performance standards — measurable goals.
  2. Measuring actual performance — data collection.
  3. Comparing performance with standards — variance analysis.
  4. Analyzing deviations — root cause analysis.
  5. Taking corrective action — to bring performance back on track.

Effective controlling requires timely, accurate information and flexibility.

Q20Explain the types of control in management. (5 Marks)

Ans:

  • Feedforward/Pre-control: Anticipates problems before they occur (e.g., recruitment standards).
  • Concurrent/Steering control: Monitors ongoing processes in real-time (e.g., quality checks).
  • Feedback/Post-control: Evaluates results after completion (e.g., financial audits).

Other classifications include financial control, budgetary control, and non-financial/operational control.

Q21What are the principles of ethics in business? (5 Marks)

Ans: Key principles of business ethics:

  • Integrity: Honest and fair conduct.
  • Transparency: Openness in dealings.
  • Accountability: Accepting responsibility for actions.
  • Fairness: Treating all stakeholders justly.
  • Respect for stakeholders: Considering all parties' interests.
  • Sustainability: Protecting environment and future generations.
Q22Discuss the sources and types of conflict in organizations. (5 Marks)

Ans:

Sources of conflict: Communication breakdown, structural issues, personal differences, incompatible goals, scarce resources.

Types of conflict:

  • Intrapersonal: Within an individual.
  • Interpersonal: Between two individuals.
  • Intragroup: Within a team.
  • Intergroup: Between teams/departments.
  • Inter-organizational: Between organizations.
Q23What is Knowledge Management? List its processes. (5 Marks)

Ans: Knowledge Management (KM) is the process of capturing, storing, sharing, and effectively using organizational knowledge.

Processes:

  1. Knowledge creation/acquisition
  2. Knowledge storage (databases, documents)
  3. Knowledge sharing (communities of practice)
  4. Knowledge application (use in decision-making)

It converts tacit (personal) knowledge to explicit (documented) knowledge.

Q24Explain the causes and management of stress. (5 Marks)

Ans:

Causes of stress: Work overload, role conflict, role ambiguity, poor working conditions, lack of autonomy, interpersonal conflicts, organizational change.

Stress management techniques:

  • Clear role definition and goals
  • Time management training
  • Relaxation techniques (yoga, meditation)
  • Counseling and employee assistance programs
  • Improving physical work environment
  • Job redesign and enrichment
Q25Describe the process of Communication. (5 Marks)

Ans: Communication process steps:

  1. Sender — has an idea.
  2. Encoding — converts idea into message.
  3. Message — content transmitted.
  4. Channel — medium (verbal, written, electronic).
  5. Decoding — receiver interprets message.
  6. Receiver — receives and understands.
  7. Feedback — receiver's response.
  8. Noise — distortion at any step.

Group C — Long Answer Questions (15 Marks Each)

Q1Explain in detail the various functions of management with a suitable diagram. (15 Marks)

Ans:

The functions of management are the fundamental activities that managers perform to achieve organizational goals. They form a continuous cycle.

graph TD A[Planning] --> B[Organizing] B --> C[Staffing] C --> D[Directing] D --> E[Controlling] E --> F[Coordination] F --> A

1. Planning: The primary function. It involves setting objectives and deciding on courses of action. Includes forecasting, goal-setting, strategy formulation, and developing budgets.

2. Organizing: Establishing a structure of authority and responsibility. Includes designing jobs, grouping tasks, allocating resources, and creating reporting relationships.

3. Staffing: Filling positions with qualified people. Includes HR planning, recruitment, selection, training, and performance appraisal.

4. Directing: Activating people to perform. Includes leadership, motivation, communication, and supervision to guide employees toward goals.

5. Controlling: Ensuring performance matches plans. Includes setting standards, measuring actual performance, comparing, and correcting deviations.

6. Coordination: The binding function. Integrates activities across departments to achieve harmony and unity of action.

Q2Discuss the Classical Management Approaches — Taylor, Fayol, and Weber. (15 Marks)

Ans:

The classical approach emphasizes structure, efficiency, and rules. It has three sub-schools:

1. Scientific Management — F.W. Taylor:

  • Father of Scientific Management.
  • Principles: Science not rule of thumb; Harmony not discord; Cooperation not individualism; Development of each person.
  • Tools: Time-and-motion studies; Standardization; Differential piece-wage system; Mental revolution.

2. Administrative Management — Henri Fayol:

  • Father of General/Administrative Management.
  • Identified 14 principles of management and 6 activities of any business (technical, commercial, financial, security, accounting, managerial).

3. Bureaucratic Management — Max Weber:

  • Ideal bureaucracy: Hierarchy, rules, specialization, impersonal relations, technical competence.
  • Strength: predictability, consistency, efficiency.
  • Criticism: Rigidity, red tape, dehumanization.

Classical approach laid the foundation for modern management theory.

Q3Explain the Planning Process with a suitable diagram. Discuss types of plans. (15 Marks)

Ans:

flowchart TD A[Set Objectives] --> B[Develop Premises] B --> C[Identify Alternatives] C --> D[Evaluate Alternatives] D --> E[Select Best Plan] E --> F[Implement Plan] F --> G[Follow-up & Revise] G --> A

Types of Plans:

  • Strategic plans: Long-term, organization-wide, set by top management.
  • Tactical plans: Medium-term, departmental, translate strategy into action.
  • Operational plans: Short-term, day-to-day, specific actions.
  • Single-use plans: Developed for unique events (budgets, projects).
  • Standing plans: Used repeatedly (policies, rules, procedures).

Planning bridges the gap between where we are and where we want to be.

Q4Describe different types of Organizational Structures with diagrams. (15 Marks)

Ans:

1. Functional Structure:

graph TD CEO[CEO] --> M[Marketing Manager] CEO --> F[Finance Manager] CEO --> P[Production Manager] CEO --> H[HR Manager]

Groups by function. Strength: specialization. Weakness: poor coordination across functions.

2. Divisional Structure:

graph TD CEO[CEO] --> D1[Division A] CEO --> D2[Division B] CEO --> D3[Division C] D1 --> M1[Marketing] D1 --> F1[Finance]

Groups by product, customer, or geography. Strength: focus, accountability. Weakness: duplication of resources.

3. Matrix Structure:

graph TD FM[Functional Manager] --> E1[Employee A] PM[Project Manager] --> E1 FM --> E2[Employee B] PM --> E2

Dual reporting: functional + project. Strength: flexibility, expertise sharing. Weakness: conflict, confusion.

4. Line and Staff: Combines line authority with staff specialists who advise.

5. Committee Organization: Groups experts for advice and coordination.

Q5Explain Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs with a diagram. Compare it with Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory. (15 Marks)

Ans:

graph BT A[Self-Actualization: Growth, fulfillment] --> B[Esteem: Status, respect] B --> C[Social: Love, belonging] C --> D[Safety: Security, stability] D --> E[Physiological: Food, shelter, sleep]

Maslow's Hierarchy:

  1. Physiological: Basic survival needs.
  2. Safety: Physical and economic security.
  3. Social: Belonging, affection.
  4. Esteem: Self-respect and recognition.
  5. Self-actualization: Realizing one's potential.

A satisfied need no longer motivates. The lowest unsatisfied need drives behavior.

Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory:

  • Hygiene factors (salary, policies, supervision) — prevent dissatisfaction.
  • Motivation factors (achievement, recognition, growth) — drive satisfaction.

Comparison: Maslow is content-based (what needs people have); Herzberg is process-based (what satisfies/dissatisfies). Herzberg's hygiene roughly maps to Maslow's lower needs; motivation factors map to esteem and self-actualization.

Q6Discuss the Decision Making Process and various Decision Making Techniques. (15 Marks)

Ans:

flowchart TD A[Identify Problem] --> B[Diagnose Problem] B --> C[Develop Alternatives] C --> D[Evaluate Alternatives] D --> E[Select Best Alternative] E --> F[Implement Decision] F --> G[Evaluate Results]

Types of Decisions: Programmed vs Non-programmed; Strategic, Tactical, Operational; Structured vs Unstructured.

Decision Making Techniques:

  • Qualitative: Experience, judgment, intuition, group consensus.
  • Quantitative: Marginal analysis, cost-benefit analysis, break-even analysis, linear programming, decision trees, PERT/CPM, queuing theory, simulation, game theory.
  • Modern tools: Decision Support Systems (DSS), Artificial Intelligence, expert systems, analytics.

Effective decision-making combines rational analysis with creative thinking.

Q7Explain the Staffing process and the various techniques of Training and Development. (15 Marks)

Ans:

Staffing Process:

  1. Human Resource Planning — Forecast demand & supply of people.
  2. Recruitment — Attract candidates (internal/external).
  3. Selection — Choose the best candidate (interviews, tests).
  4. Placement & Orientation — Assigning job and familiarizing.
  5. Training & Development — Improving skills.
  6. Performance Appraisal — Evaluating performance.
  7. Promotion & Career Planning — Growth opportunities.

Training Techniques:

  • On-the-job: Job rotation, coaching, mentoring, apprenticeship, committee assignments.
  • Off-the-job: Lectures, case studies, role-playing, simulation, sensitivity training, vestibule training.

Effective staffing ensures the right people are in the right jobs at the right time.

Q8Discuss Leadership Styles and Theories (Trait, Behavioral, Contingency). (15 Marks)

Ans:

1. Trait Theory: Leaders have certain innate qualities (confidence, intelligence, decisiveness, integrity). It assumes leaders are born, not made. Limitation: doesn't list universal traits.

2. Behavioral Theory:

  • Ohio State Studies: Initiating Structure vs Consideration.
  • Michigan Studies: Production-oriented vs Employee-oriented.
  • Managerial Grid (Blake & Mouton): Concern for People vs Concern for Production — best is "Team Management" (9,9).

3. Contingency Theory:

  • Fiedler: Effectiveness depends on leader-style fit with situation (leader-member relations, task structure, position power).
  • Hersey-Blanchard (Situational): Style should match follower maturity (Telling, Selling, Participating, Delegating).
  • Path-Goal: Leader clarifies path to goals — Directive, Supportive, Participative, Achievement-oriented.

Modern view: leadership is contextual — different situations need different leaders.

Q9Explain the Communication Process and discuss the types and barriers of communication. (15 Marks)

Ans:

flowchart LR S[Sender] -->|Encoding| M[Message] M -->|Channel| N[Noise] N -->|Decoding| RV[Receiver] RV -->|Feedback| S

Types of Communication:

  • Formal vs Informal (grapevine).
  • Verbal vs Non-verbal (body language, gestures).
  • Upward, Downward, Horizontal, Diagonal.
  • Written vs Oral.
  • Internal vs External.

Barriers: Semantic (words), physical (noise), psychological (perception, bias), cultural, organizational (status, complexity), personal.

Improving communication: Active listening, feedback, clarity, choosing the right channel, empathy, simplifying language.

Q10Discuss the Controlling function in detail, including Budgetary Control techniques. (15 Marks)

Ans:

Controlling Process:

  1. Establish performance standards.
  2. Measure actual performance.
  3. Compare actual vs standard.
  4. Determine deviations.
  5. Analyze causes of deviations.
  6. Take corrective action.

Types of Control: Feedforward, Concurrent, Feedback (Post-control).

Budgetary Control:

  • Types of budgets: Sales, production, cash, capital, master budget.
  • Steps: Set objectives → Prepare budgets → Record actuals → Compare → Analyze variances → Take corrective action.
  • Advantages: Defines responsibility, enables evaluation, aids planning.
  • Limitations: Rigidity, focus on quantitative only, may not handle non-suit.

Other control techniques: PERT, Statistical Process Control, ROI, ratio analysis.

Q11Explain Change Management and the process of Organizational Development (OD). (15 Marks)

Ans:

flowchart TD A[Recognize Need for Change] --> B[Diagnose Problem] B --> C[Plan Change] C --> D[Implement Change] D --> E[Evaluate Results] E --> F[Reinforce Change]

Types of Change: Planned vs Unplanned; Incremental vs Transformational; Technology, Structure, People.

Forces of Change: External (technology, market, competition, regulation) and Internal (strategy, workforce, attitude).

Resistance to Change: Fear, habit, economic factors, security, selective information processing.

Overcoming Resistance: Education, communication, participation, facilitation, negotiation, coercion.

Organizational Development (OD): A long-term, planned process using behavioral science to improve organization effectiveness and health.

OD Interventions: Sensitivity training, survey feedback, process consultation, team building, intergroup development, MBO, job redesign.

Q12Discuss Corporate Governance, Ethics, Organizational Culture, and Knowledge Management. (15 Marks)

Ans:

1. Corporate Governance:

  • System of directing and controlling companies.
  • Components: Board of Directors, shareholders, management, auditors, regulators.
  • Principles: Transparency, accountability, fairness, responsibility.

2. Business Ethics:

  • Principles guiding behavior in business.
  • Importance: Builds trust, ensures sustainability, avoids legal issues, enhances reputation.

3. Organizational Culture:

  • Shared values, beliefs, norms, customs.
  • Functions: Provides identity, facilitates commitment, stabilizes social system, shapes behavior.
  • Types: Clan, Adhocracy, Market, Hierarchy (Cameron & Quinn).

4. Knowledge Management (KM):

  • Capture, store, share, and use organizational knowledge.
  • Types of knowledge: Tacit (experience-based) vs Explicit (documented).
  • Tools: Databases, communities of practice, mentoring, lessons-learned systems, AI.

Together, these shape an organization's long-term sustainability and competitive advantage.
